2. An improved GPY sieve method

We first give an explanation of the key idea behind our new approach. The basic idea of the GPY method is, for a fixed admissible set ℋ={h1,…,hk}\mathcal{H}=\{h_{1},\dotsc,h_{k}\}, to consider the sum

(2.1)

S⁡(N,ρ)\displaystyle S(N,\rho)
=∑N≤n<2​N(∑i=1kχℙ​(n+hi)−ρ)​wn.\displaystyle=\sum_{N\leq n<2N}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\chi_{\mathbb{P}}(n+h_{i})-\rho\Bigr)w_{n}.

Here χℙ\chi_{\mathbb{P}} is the characteristic function of the primes, ρ>0\rho>0 and wnw_{n} are non-negative weights. If we can show that S⁡(N,ρ)>0S(N,\rho)>0 then at least one term in the sum over nn must have a positive contribution. By the non-negativity of wnw_{n}, this means that there must be some integer n∈[N,2​N]n\in[N,2N] such that at least ⌊ρ+1⌋\lfloor\rho+1\rfloor of the n+hin+h_{i} are prime. (Here ⌊x⌋\lfloor x\rfloor denotes the largest integer less than or equal to xx.) Thus if S⁡(N,ρ)>0S(N,\rho)>0 for all large NN, there are infinitely many integers nn for which at least ⌊ρ+1⌋\lfloor\rho+1\rfloor of the n+hin+h_{i} are prime (and so there are infinitely many bounded length intervals containing ⌊ρ+1⌋\lfloor\rho+1\rfloor primes).

The weights wnw_{n} are typically chosen to mimic Selberg sieve weights. Estimating (2.1) can be interpreted as a ‘kk-dimensional’ sieve problem. The standard Selberg kk-dimensional weights (which can be shown to be essentially optimal in other contexts) are

With this choice we find that we just fail to prove the existence of bounded gaps between primes if we assume the Elliott-Halberstam conjecture. The key new idea in the paper of Goldston, Pintz and Yıldırım [5] was to consider more general sieve weights of the form

(2.3)

λd=μ⁡(d)​F​(log⁡R/d),\lambda_{d}=\mu(d)F(\log{R/d}),

for a suitable smooth function FF. Goldston, Pintz and Yıldırım chose F⁡(x)=xk+lF(x)=x^{k+l} for suitable l∈ℕl\in\mathbb{N}, which has been shown to be essentially optimal when kk is large. This allows us to gain a factor of approximately 22 for large kk over the previous choice of sieve weights. As a result we just fail to prove bounded gaps using the fact that the primes have exponent of distribution θ\theta for any θ<1/2\theta<1/2, but succeed in doing so if we assume they have level of distribution θ>1/2\theta>1/2.

The new ingredient in our method is to consider a more general form of the sieve weights

(2.4)

wn=(∑di|n+hi​∀iλd1,…,dk)2.w_{n}=\Bigl(\sum_{d_{i}|n+h_{i}\forall i}\lambda_{d_{1},\dotsc,d_{k}}\Bigr)^{2}.

It allows us to improve on the previous choice of sieve weights by an arbitrarily large factor, provided that kk is sufficiently large. It is the extra flexibility gained by allowing the weights to depend on the divisors of each factor individually which gives this improvement.

The idea to use such weights is not entirely new. Selberg [8, Page 245] suggested the possible use of similar weights in his work on approximations to the twin prime problem, and Goldston and Yıldırım [6] considered similar weights in earlier work on the GPY method, but with the support restricted to di<R1/kd_{i}<R^{1/k} for all ii.

for a suitable smooth function ff. For our precise choice of λd1,…,dk\lambda_{d_{1},\dots,d_{k}} (given in Proposition 4.1) we find it convenient to give a slightly different form of λd1,…,dk\lambda_{d_{1},\dots,d_{k}}, but weights of the form (2.5) should produce essentially the same results.
